How a three-part offer works?

Thermal units sell into ERCOT's day-ahead market (DAM) with a three-part offer. The DAM picks which units to commit for each hour of the next day at least total cost, so it weighs all three parts together across the whole day, not hour by hour. An awarded unit is paid the day-ahead price, not its offer.

① Start-up cost
A lump sum per start, in three versions by how long the unit has been off (hot, intermediate, cold). A unit already running pays none, so a start cost makes staying on through cheap hours cheaper than stopping and restarting.
② Minimum-energy offer
The $/MWh price for running at minimum output (LSL, the low sustained limit). This is the part that decides cheap hours: offer it near $0 and the DAM keeps the unit on; offer $20 to $30 and the DAM usually lets it go.
③ Energy curve
Prices for each block of output above LSL, up to the unit's maximum (HSL). It sets how far the unit is dispatched up once it is on.
Terms used below
Cheap hours
Hours with a DA system lambda at or below $15. A day with any is a cheap day; its cheap hours are the cheap stretch.
Kept on (awarded through the stretch)
The DAM awarded the unit energy in at least half of the cheap hours.
Fuel-cost line
EIA's average full-load heat rate for the unit type times the gas price at the unit's own hub. An offer below it is an offer below fuel cost.
Implied heat rate
The minimum-energy offer divided by the hub gas price, in MMBtu/MWh.
Configuration and train
A combined cycle train (say 2 gas turbines and a steam turbine) offers each running mode as a separate configuration and runs one at a time. Fleet counts are by train; unit charts show each configuration.
Offer change
A minimum-energy offer that moves $5 or more and holds, a short price-out, a start-cost change, or a unit starting or stopping three-part offers. Major changes cross the fuel-cost line or fall on cheap days.
