Floor-priced MW by technology

Wind and solar offer prices

Share of the MW wind and solar offered each day that was priced in each band, weighted by MW over the day's hours. Tax-credit bids cluster between about −$60 and −$25 with little between −$25 and −$5, so −$20 separates them from shallow negatives. Uses the 2-day curves where the day has them, otherwise the 60-day curves.

Wind and solar curtailment

Energy wind and solar could have produced (HSL) minus what SCED dispatched them to (base point). The 60-day disclosure (solid) and ERCOT's 2-day reports (dashed: generation summary base points against the 2-day offer curves' summed HSL) report it separately. Available MW agree almost exactly; on some days the 2-day base points run a few percent lower, so the dashed line reads higher. The lower chart uses the 60-day figure where it exists and the 2-day one otherwise, and averages each hour's curtailed MW by that hour's system lambda: the cheap energy that was available but not taken.

Curtailment at the node

The same curtailed energy priced at each unit's own settlement point. An hour counts as operator-directed where ERCOT's HDL/LDL Manual Override Summary shows an operator lowering the unit's HDL (curtailment up to that reduction, averaged over the hour's runs), as congestion when the node price is at least $5/MWh below system lambda (the energy was worth less where the unit sits, often negative) and as oversupply otherwise (the system as a whole did not want it at the unit's offer). The split is by price, so it says what the energy was worth at the node, not which constraint SCED was managing. Units are matched to nodes through the DAM disclosure; hours with no node price are priced at lambda and counted as unmatched. Node prices load a few days per update, newest first, so early days fill in over time.

Thermal offer prices

Price at which 25%, 50% and 90% of a technology's submitted MW is offered, averaged over the day's hours. A falling median with a steady lambda means units are bidding more of their capacity cheaply. Nuclear submits no curves.

Natural gas and implied heat rate

Henry Hub daily spot price from the EIA, and the median submitted offer price of combined cycles and combustion turbines divided by that day's gas price: the heat rate (MMBtu per MWh) the offers imply if they were pure fuel cost. A steady implied heat rate with a moving offer price means the offers are tracking gas; a changing one means something else moved (margins, variable costs, the mix of units offering). Henry Hub is used in place of the Houston Ship Channel price, which usually trades near it.

Marginal supply by settled price

Which resources move when the price moves, across every hour loaded, sorted by that hour's system lambda. Above zero: each technology's share of the supply MW whose offer price falls just around lambda. Below zero: battery charging bids that stop at that price, as a share of supply plus charging. Smoothed blends the 100 hours nearest each price, so each point rests on the same amount of data. Exact uses only the hours in each $1 band of lambda.
