How it's built

Where the fleet's offers sit, from each battery's submitted curve: charging stops is the highest price at which it still bids to charge, half the charging the price below which it bids at least half its charging range; discharge starts is the lowest price at which it offers any discharge, half the discharge the price at which its offer reaches half its HSL. Each is the battery's median over the hour's SCED runs, then the median across batteries weighted by their size. The grey lines are the hub price, real-time (solid) and day-ahead (dashed). Room left unused

Room the fleet had to discharge (above zero) or charge (below) but didn't use, split into ancillary service holdback and unused.

How it's built

How much more the fleet could have discharged (above zero) or charged (below) within its telemetered limits, which already reflect state of charge. Part is held for ancillary services awarded in SCED (Reg-Up, RRS, ECRS and Non-Spin above, Reg-Down below); the rest was free and not used, because the battery's own offer priced it out. A full battery has no charging room and an empty one no discharge room, so neither shows here.

Across days

How big the fleet is, how hard it works, and how much of the best possible energy revenue it earned, against a perfect battery that knew the day's real-time prices in advance. The marker is the chosen day.

How it's built

Every battery of 5 MW or more that reports a state of charge and has a real-time price at its settlement point, each day. Fleet size sums their largest HSL (power) and their usable state of charge range, maximum less minimum (energy). Full cycles is the energy they discharged (telemetered output) over that range. Energy earned is two-settlement: the day-ahead award at the day-ahead price at the battery's node, plus output less the award at the real-time node price, hourly. Day-ahead AS earned is each day-ahead AS award at the day-ahead clearing price; real-time AS changes it by little and is left out. The perfect battery is, for each battery, the best schedule against that day's hourly real-time node prices known in advance: same MW, same usable range, its own round-trip efficiency (fitted from how its state of charge moves, about 86% for the median battery), the MW it actually held for AS taken out of its limits, the day starting at its actual state of charge and ending at least at its actual end-of-day state of charge, and at most one full cycle a day. Hourly prices understate what a 15-minute perfect battery could earn. The share earned is energy earned over the perfect battery's energy revenue; single days swing widely, so the 7-day line sums both over the week first. Charged at and discharged at are MWh-weighted real-time node prices of what the fleet charged and discharged, against the same for the perfect battery (dashed).

Source: ERCOT EMIL products NP3-965-ER (60-Day SCED Disclosure: the ESR file's submitted curves, base points, limits, state of charge and AS awards), NP3-966-ER (60-Day DAM Disclosure: the ESR Data file's energy and AS awards and the AS clearing prices), the real-time hub and resource node prices and the DAM settlement point prices. Built by the data update workflow from the days already loaded; the 60-day reports run about two months behind.
