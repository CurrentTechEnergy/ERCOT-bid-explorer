Wind and solar curtailment

Energy wind and solar could have produced (HSL) minus what SCED dispatched them to (base point). The 60-day disclosure (solid) and ERCOT's 2-day reports (dashed: generation summary base points against the 2-day offer curves' summed HSL) report it separately. Available MW agree almost exactly; on some days the 2-day base points run a few percent lower, so the dashed line reads higher. The middle chart uses the 60-day figure where it exists and the 2-day one otherwise, and averages each hour's curtailed MW by that hour's system lambda: the cheap energy that was available but not taken.

One day by hour

MW wind and solar could have produced (HSL) minus the base point SCED sent them, averaged over each hour: the output SCED held back, for price or for congestion. From the 60-day disclosure, unit by unit; recent days with no 60-day data yet use an estimate from ERCOT's 2-day reports (the generation summary's base points against the top of the 2-day offer curves, since the summary no longer carries available output).

Curtailment at the node

The same curtailed energy priced at each unit's own settlement point. An hour counts as operator-directed where ERCOT's HDL/LDL Manual Override Summary shows an operator lowering the unit's HDL (curtailment up to that reduction, averaged over the hour's runs), as congestion when the node price is at least $5/MWh below system lambda (the energy was worth less where the unit sits, often negative) and as oversupply otherwise (the system as a whole did not want it at the unit's offer). The split is by price, so it says what the energy was worth at the node, not which constraint SCED was managing. Units are matched to nodes through the DAM disclosure; hours with no node price are priced at lambda and counted as unmatched. Node prices load a few days per update, newest first, so early days fill in over time.

How the hedge signals and changes are found, and reading them with care

How it works

Per MWh, a plant hedged on a DA-settled, as-generated contract earns roughly the real-time price at its node, plus share × (strike − DA price at the settlement point), plus any per-MWh credit. Generating pays only when the RT price beats share × DA − (share × strike + credit), and that is the offer a QSE bidding the contract submits. Each hour's offer is the median over its SCED runs of the submitted curve's price at half its MW, kept only when it did not move within the hour (a DA-based price is hourly). A formula day has at least four such hours at four or more prices, after dropping a floor or cap the formula is clamped to. Every candidate settlement point (the unit's own node, each hub and load zone) is fitted on each formula day; the point's formula is the median over days, and its hit rate is the share of hours it predicts within $2.

How changes are found

Reading it with care

Most units never show a formula: a flat offer at −$30 or $0 says what the unit gives up by not running, not how it is hedged, and physical, as-available or fixed-shape contracts leave no trace in offers at all. A formula that only appears on high-price days (Outpost and Frye during Winter Storm Fern) is a policy the operator switched on, not a standing rule. The slope is the share of the bidder's exposure tied to that DA price, which need not match the contracted share: Phoebe's public hedge covers 89% of output, while its offers follow 100% of HB_NORTH. Where a unit's node prices like a hub or load zone, the fit cannot tell them apart, and the tier says so.
