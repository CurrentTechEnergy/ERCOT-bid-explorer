Wind and solar offer prices

Share of wind and solar MW offered in each price band, day by day. Tax-credit bids sit between about −$60 and −$20.

How it's built

Share of the MW wind and solar offered each day that was priced in each band, weighted by MW over the day's hours. Tax-credit bids cluster between about −$60 and −$25 with little between −$25 and −$5, so −$20 separates them from shallow negatives. Below −$20 is split into −$250 to −$50, −$50 to −$35 and −$35 to −$20, since most of it sits between −$50 and −$30. Almost nothing is offered at the −$250 floor itself. Uses the 2-day curves where the day has them, otherwise the 60-day curves.

Wind and solar curtailment

Curtailed energy by day, split into operator-directed, congestion (node at least $5 under lambda) and oversupply.

How it's built

Energy wind and solar could have produced (HSL) minus what SCED dispatched them to (base point), hour by hour, from the 60-day disclosure unit by unit. Each curtailed hour is priced at the unit's own settlement point and counted as operator-directed where ERCOT's HDL/LDL Manual Override Summary shows an operator lowering the unit's HDL (curtailment up to that reduction, averaged over the hour's runs), as congestion when the node price is at least $5/MWh below system lambda (the energy was worth less where the unit sits, often negative) and as oversupply otherwise (the system as a whole did not want it at the unit's offer). The split is by price, so it says what the energy was worth at the node, not which constraint SCED was managing. Units are matched to nodes through the DAM disclosure; hours with no node price are priced at lambda and counted as unmatched. The price chart groups the curtailed energy by each hour's system lambda (hover for the average curtailed MW over those hours) or by the price at the unit's node, which leaves out operator-directed energy. Recent days with no 60-day data yet show a dashed total estimated from ERCOT's 2-day reports (generation summary base points against the 2-day offer curves' HSL); where both exist the 2-day estimate runs a few percent higher, because the 2-day base points run a little lower.

Units

Units whose offers track a day-ahead price. “Offers at” is the formula they follow; the implied strike is what that formula suggests the hedge is struck at. Click a unit to open it.

How it's built

Click a column to sort and a unit to open its page, with its fit and bidding history. Share is the slope of offer on the DA price, Intercept its constant. The strike is a range: the high end assumes the intercept is all strike, the low end that the operator also counts a credit of about $30/MWh (roughly the PTC) in its break-even. Floor is the unit's most common offer; DA share is its DAM energy award as a share of its real-time base point energy.
