All units

Type a unit, plant, county or settlement point in the Unit box above to filter, and click a unit to open its page. Thermal units, wind, solar and batteries are listed; a combined-cycle train is one entry, and a search for one of its configurations finds it. Group is the unit's timescale group in the latest forecast (how often it shuts down; thermal only). Capacity is the 95th percentile of each day's highest HSL. Name, county and zone come from ERCOT's Capacity, Demand and Reserves report, matched on the unit code; plant marks a unit matched only to its plant, which is how combined-cycle trains match, since the report lists each turbine. The Unit box also searches these names. Batteries renamed for RTC+B (names ending _ESR1) mostly match the report only by plant.

How it's built

What the site's detectors found changed for this unit: shutdowns that broke its pattern, moves in or out of daily cycling, shifts in how it bids, cutoff moves, wind and solar floor or formula changes, long outages, and for batteries lasting size changes and the first day online. A unit's first day in SCED shows here too. "Found" is the day the update first saw it; unit data runs about sixty days behind the operating day.

Forecast by hour

The forecast for this unit by hour: odds online, expected and minimum MW, against the day-ahead price. Actuals are drawn over it once known.

How it's built

What the forecast expected of this unit on one day, hour by hour: its chance of being online and the on/off call (on when that chance is 50% or more), the MW it expected and the minimum it held the unit at, against the day-ahead price it was judged on (its own node, else the hub average). Once the day's 60-day data is in, what the unit actually did is drawn over it. The loss window, where one is shaded, is the day's worst stretch for staying on, the hours the call is about.

For combined cycle and gas steam units, once the 60-day data is in (about 61 days after the day), a second row shows the call made with what the operator knew going in: whether the unit was running at the end of the day before and how long it had been, its day-ahead award and resource status, the day-ahead price against its own costs and against gas cost, and its own habit. Each hour uses only what came before it. Hours with a day-ahead award are shaded. The diamonds are the unit's Current Operating Plan as of 2 pm the day before, shown beside the call rather than used in it, since the plan is the operator writing down the decision itself. The reasons are for one hour: the unit's first actual switch that day, else the hour the call switches, else its cheapest hour.

Decision history

Each point is a day the unit started online: what staying on through the cheap stretch would have cost it, and whether it stayed on or shut down.

How it's built

Each point is a day the unit started online, with what the day-ahead prices at its node said it would lose by staying on through the day's worst stretch (energy at its node price against its costs at minimum output), and what it did. The forecast's cutoff for the unit is the loss that best separates its shutdowns from its stays.

With operator info

The same days judged with what the operator knew going in (its state, day-ahead award and status, the price against its own cost and its gas cost, and its own habit): the chance it stays online through the stretch. A shutdown (red) above 50% or a stay (open) below it is a day the operator-informed call got wrong.

One day by hour

What the battery did hour by hour: discharging above zero and charging below it from the base points SCED sent, averaged over each hour, against its day-ahead award (dashed). Below, its state of charge with its minimum and maximum, then the offer prices from its submitted curve against the day-ahead price at its node: charging stops is the highest price at which it still bids to charge, discharge starts the lowest price at which it offers to discharge, and the dashed lines the prices for half its charging and half its discharge range. Each is the median over the hour's SCED runs.

Across days

Energy charged and discharged each day against its day-ahead awards, and its offer prices each day: the price where its charging stops, averaged over HE 10 to 16, and the lowest price at which it offered discharge in any hour. Batteries rebid all the time, so read the trend, not a single day. Click a day to show it above.
